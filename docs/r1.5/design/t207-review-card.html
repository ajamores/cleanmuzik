CleanMuzik/R1.5 · T-207 review card/ADR-016 design gate

What the review card says, and why

R1.5 adds a park story to the review card: a one-line reason it stopped, the contradictions between the three senses that couldn't agree, and the candidates in the order the adjudicator ranked them. These screens show that story across every state it lands in — including the two where there's no card at all.

STATIC MOCKS  Flat screens, not a working app — one per state, including the edges. Nothing is clickable. Look, then sign off; component code comes after (workflow.md).
DELIBERATELY ABSENT  No match percentage, and no Shazam pre-fill hint — the standalone hint is deferred to T-212. Match strength is an honest bar, never a raw score dressed as a verdict (T-017); the adjudicator's confidence is never shown because it never reaches the card.

02

How Shazam actually surfaces — the ISRC candidate

When Shazam returns an official ISRC that resolves to a real MusicBrainz recording, it joins the list as a selectable candidate, badged via Shazam — the Pa Salieu Frontline case. This is the only way Shazam reaches the card (there's no standalone hint — T-212). It parks because the video was uploaded under a channel name, so the YouTube title can't corroborate Shazam and only one sense backs the right match — you accept it through the normal path, and it lands the corrected identity. (If the title had read "Pa Salieu — Frontline", YouTube would agree with Shazam — two senses — and it would auto-land instead, as in screen 5a.)

05

No review card — the two ways a track lands silently

Not every track earns a card. When two of three senses agree, the corrected identity auto-lands and never enters the queue (the Pa Salieu override). And when the adjudicator is switched off entirely — no API key — the pipeline degrades to R1's fingerprint-only gate: a strong fingerprint lands as it always did, with no reason or contradictions because nothing adjudicated it. Both shown here so sign-off covers the absence, not just the presence.
