CleanMuzik · The skin — a separate decision from the flow

Three visual directions

Same screen, three identities. Each shows CleanMuzik's real surface — paste bar, a running job, the review inbox, a landed track — so you're judging the look, not the layout. Each direction is shown in the theme it was designed around; its light/dark palette sits beside it.

Type is approximated. I can't inline the licensed fonts here, so Signal Path (IBM Plex) and Pressing Plant (Archivo Expanded) fall back to system faces and lose some character; Card Catalogue's Georgia/Courier render nearly true. At build the named face gets inlined. Palette, surfaces, badges, the rail, and each signature move render faithfully — that's where the identity actually lives.

1 Signal Path Recommended

A broadcast engineer's rack unit — software that behaves like a well-made piece of audio hardware. Dark is its native mode.

Type
IBM Plex Sans + Plex Mono for all data (durations, bitrates, scores) — engineered, calibrated.
Signature move
The progress rail is a segmented meter — discrete blocks fill left-to-right, the active one breathes, then the whole rail settles to green.
Risk
Can read cold; tips into skeuomorphic cosplay if the hardware metaphor spreads past the type and the meter.

2 Pressing Plant

A small record-pressing workshop — flat spot-ink print, work orders travelling through the plant. Paper is its native mode.

Type
Archivo Expanded Black, uppercase, for the title & headers only; JetBrains Mono for catalog data. Poster-confident, mid-century label print.
Signature move
The stamp — LANDED / DUPLICATE impresses once with a slight rotation; the active job card carries a hard offset ink shadow (misregistration).
Risk
Loud. Expanded caps + thick borders fatigue fast if they spread past the header and the one primary card.

3 Card Catalogue Best for the inbox

A municipal library's accession ledger — the tool as a patient archivist filing records into the collection. Reading-room light is its native mode.

Type
System sans for the chrome; Charter serif for the records themselves (titles, artists) — the sans/serif split is the whole idea. Courier for accession numbers.
Signature move
The verdict stamp — resolving a review impresses FILED onto the row and it persists in the item's history. State becomes a physical record.
Risk
One bad call from twee (fake paper grain, typewriter everything). Works only held to the cool palette and the strict sans-chrome / serif-content split.

Pick one to build against. My read matches Fable's: Signal Path for the calmest daily driver — the mono-data discipline fits your durations/bitrates/scores exactly, and the meter gives the progress rail a native language. Card Catalogue if you'd rather it feel loved than professional; its stamp metaphor is the strongest fit for the review inbox itself. Pressing Plant is the most memorable and the most likely to wear out its welcome.
