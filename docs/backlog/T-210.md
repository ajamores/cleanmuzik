# T-210 — MusicBrainz rate limiting + per-call timeout (the tail-capping speed lever)

**Status:** **PRIMARY remaining speed lever (2026-08-24).** Two related pieces now live here: (1) the
original shared-rate-limiter finding, and (2) a **per-call MB timeout** — promoted to the top time lever
now that [`T-208`](T-208.md) has shipped. T-208 killed the MB *call-count* problem (11→2 hydrations); the
**per-call latency tail is what's left**, and it's the #1 remaining time cost. The T-208 acceptance compare
showed wall-clock is now noisy-not-fast because any single MB call can still hang (the live playlist spiked
one track to ~98s while others sat at ~25s; T-218 measured individual `_get_resource` calls at 21s and 34s).
Cap the tail and wall-clock becomes predictable. Cheap, non-engine-identity, ADR-safe.
**Found:** 2026-08-10 (T-203 code-review, PLAUSIBLE finding — high-effort workflow review)

## The per-call timeout (the speed half, 2026-08-24)

Wrap every MB fetch (`MusicBrainzAPI._get_resource`, the one HTTP chokepoint) in a bounded timeout (e.g.
5s). On expiry: treat as a miss on that call — a candidate hydration that times out drops that candidate; a
year-stamp that times out lands without the year (ADR-014 already tolerates a missing year). Never crash,
never block the batch. This is the orthogonal lever T-218 flagged: it doesn't lower the median, it kills the
18–34s spikes that make a landing *feel* broken. Build it first — it's the cheapest real time win left.

## Problem

`app/isrc.py` throttles its direct MusicBrainz calls with its **own** module-level 1/sec
gate (`_respect_rate_limit`). beets throttles its MusicBrainz candidate calls with a
**separate** `LimiterTimeoutSession` (`per_second=1.0`). The two share no state.

Within one track's serial processing, beets fires an MB candidate-generation call and then
`isrc_to_mb` fires immediately after — its own gate is empty on this track, so it does not
wait. Two requests can hit `musicbrainz.org` inside the same second, breaching the host's
global 1 req/sec floor (ADR-001). MB may answer `503`; `isrc.py` maps every non-200 to
`None`, so a genuinely resolvable ISRC — e.g. the marquee Pa Salieu `Frontline`
(`GBAHT1901215`) correction — silently misses, and the ADR-021 Rule 2 correction it exists
to feed never fires.

## Why deferred, not fixed in T-203

- The **26-track spike** ran both MB paths against live MusicBrainz with *no* throttle at
  all and never tripped MB's limiter — so the practical risk is low at single-user volume.
- A true fix means routing both paths through **one** shared limiter (or teaching the isrc
  gate about beets' last MB call), which reaches into how beets' session is wired — a real
  chunk of work, out of proportion to T-203's scope.
- The T-209 verify sweep exercises the real pipeline against the spike corpus with a live
  key; a rate-limit collision, if real, surfaces there. If T-209 shows silent ISRC misses
  clustered on back-to-back MB calls, graduate this.

## Done when

A single rate limiter governs *all* traffic to `musicbrainz.org` from one track's
processing — beets' candidate call and the ISRC lookup can never put two requests inside
one second — and the Pa Salieu correction resolves reliably under the real serial pipeline.
