# T-210 — MusicBrainz rate limiting + per-call timeout (the tail-capping speed lever)

**Status:** **SPEED HALF DONE — integrated to `main` 2026-08-25 (ADR-031).** RATE-LIMITER HALF still open.
Two independent pieces live here: (1) the **retry-ladder bound** (the speed half — done) and (2) the
original **shared-rate-limiter / ISRC** finding (correctness — open). Split them because they turned out to
be unrelated.
**Found:** 2026-08-10 (T-203 code-review, PLAUSIBLE finding — high-effort workflow review)

## The speed half — retry-ladder bound (DONE 2026-08-25, ADR-031)

**The premise this was filed on was wrong, and the fix changed with it.** The ticket said to *add* a per-call
timeout ("wrap every MB fetch in a bounded 5s timeout"). But beets' `TimeoutAndRetrySession.request` already
sets `timeout=10` per request — there is no uncapped fetch. Reading the real session showed the 18–34s (once
98s) tail is beets' **retry ladder**: the MB `RateLimitAdapter` mounts `Retry(total=6, backoff_factor=0.5,
status_forcelist=[500,502,503,504,429])` → up to six attempts with exponential backoff (~0.5→16s, summing
past 30s), each attempt also able to burn the 10s socket timeout. That is the spike T-218 measured.

**Built:** `app/mb_retry.install_bounded_mb_retries()` (installed from `configure_beets()` after
`load_plugins()`, beside the ADR-030 patches) re-stamps the adapter's `max_retries` in place via
`Retry.new(total=1)` — only the count changes; backoff, the 5xx/429 status list, the 0.25s adapter spacing,
and the session-level 1/sec limiter are preserved. One retry keeps a single transient absorb while collapsing
the deep backoff tail: the common 503-storm spike (~30s of pure backoff sleep) is gone; only a rare hung
endpoint still costs ~two socket timeouts (~20s), down from the ~30s+ ladder.

**Degradation-to-miss is inherited, not added:** after a bounded ladder exhausts, beets raises;
`_stamp_original_year` lands the track without a year, and beets' `maybe_handle_plugin_error` turns a raising
`track_for_id` into `None` (a dropped hydration → ADR-030 parks it, never lands thin). Never crashes, never
stalls the batch. Caps the tail; **does not lower the median** (a slow-but-successful call is still ≤10s; the
steady per-track floor is T-219/T-035's ground).

**Done when (speed half):** the live engine's MB session, after `configure_beets()`, carries `max_retries.total
== 1` on the MusicBrainz adapter with backoff/status-list/spacing intact, and a lookup failure degrades to a
blank year / park rather than a crash. ✅ 751 tests green (8 new in `test_mb_retry.py`); live-adapter assertion
confirmed against the real configure path.

## The rate-limiter / ISRC half (OPEN — correctness, orthogonal to the speed half)

## Problem

`app/isrc.py` throttles its direct MusicBrainz calls with its **own** module-level 1/sec
gate (`_respect_rate_limit`). beets throttles its MusicBrainz candidate calls with a
**separate** `LimiterTimeoutSession` (`per_second=1.0`). The two share no state.

Within one track's serial processing, beets fires an MB candidate-generation call and then
`isrc_to_mb` fires immediately after — its own gate is empty on this track, so it does not
wait. Two requests can hit `musicbrainz.org` inside the same second, breaching the host's
global 1 req/sec floor (ADR-001). MB may answer `503`; `isrc.py` maps every non-200 to
`None`, so a genuinely resolvable ISRC — e.g. the marquee Pa Salieu `Frontline`
(`GBAHT1901215`) correction — silently misses, and the ADR-021 Rule 2 correction it exists
to feed never fires.

## Why deferred, not fixed in T-203

- The **26-track spike** ran both MB paths against live MusicBrainz with *no* throttle at
  all and never tripped MB's limiter — so the practical risk is low at single-user volume.
- A true fix means routing both paths through **one** shared limiter (or teaching the isrc
  gate about beets' last MB call), which reaches into how beets' session is wired — a real
  chunk of work, out of proportion to T-203's scope.
- The T-209 verify sweep exercises the real pipeline against the spike corpus with a live
  key; a rate-limit collision, if real, surfaces there. If T-209 shows silent ISRC misses
  clustered on back-to-back MB calls, graduate this.

## Done when (rate-limiter half — OPEN)

A single rate limiter governs *all* traffic to `musicbrainz.org` from one track's
processing — beets' candidate call and the ISRC lookup can never put two requests inside
one second — and the Pa Salieu correction resolves reliably under the real serial pipeline.
